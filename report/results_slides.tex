\section*{Regime bias --- does normalisation help?}
\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Long\_ns (mean)} & \textbf{Long\_ns std}$\downarrow$ \\
\midrule
v14 & 0.570 & 0.343 \\
v15\_btc & 0.498 & 0.178 \\
v15\_multi & 0.616 & \bb{0.142} \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}
\par\smallskip\footnotesize Long\_ns std across the 6 folds. Lower = less polarised toward one direction per year (less regime memorisation). \bb{Blue} = best.
\end{table}
\paragraph{What it says.} This is the clearest win. v14 (no normalisation) swings violently per year --- almost 100\% long in bull years (2020/2024) and almost all short in bear years (2021/2025), giving std~$=0.297$. Adding \textbf{per-asset target normalisation alone} (v15\_btc, still BTC-only) cuts this to \textbf{0.178}, roughly halving the regime bias. Multi-asset (v15\_multi) lowers it slightly further (0.142) but at the cost of accuracy. \textbf{Conclusion: the regime-bias reduction comes from normalisation, not from adding ETH/XRP.}
